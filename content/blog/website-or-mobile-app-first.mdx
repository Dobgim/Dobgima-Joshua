**Short answer:** most businesses should build a website first, because it is how new customers find and trust you. Build a mobile app when your customers use your service often, need notifications, or need it to keep working on slow connections. Many businesses launch the website first and add the app later on the same back end.

## What each one is best at

### A website is best for being found

- Anyone can open it instantly from Google, a link or a QR code. Nothing to install.
- Search engines and AI assistants can read it and recommend you.
- It is usually faster and less expensive to build and update.
- It works on every device, from phones to laptops.

### A mobile app is best for repeat use

- It lives on the customer's home screen, one tap away.
- It can send **push notifications** to bring people back.
- It can store data on the phone and keep working when the connection is weak, then sync later.
- It can use phone features more deeply, such as the camera, location and offline storage.

## Five questions that decide it

1. **How do new customers find you?** If the answer is search, referrals or social media links, start with a website.
2. **How often do customers use your service?** Daily or weekly use, such as ordering, tracking or learning, is a strong sign an app is worth it.
3. **Do you need to send reminders or updates?** Push notifications are one of the main reasons to build an app.
4. **Will people use it with poor or expensive internet?** An app with offline support can make a real difference.
5. **What is your budget and timeline?** A website usually gets you online sooner. An app is a bigger investment that pays off with regular users.

## You do not always have to choose

A modern setup lets the website and the app share one back end: the same database, user accounts and content. I build websites with Next.js and React, and Android and iOS apps with Flutter, connected to one shared back end on Supabase or Firebase. That means:

- Customers can sign in on the website or the app with the same account.
- You manage products, bookings or content in one place.
- Adding the app later does not mean starting again.

With Flutter, one codebase produces both the Android and the iOS app, which keeps development and maintenance costs lower than building two separate apps.

## A sensible path for most businesses

1. **Launch a fast website** that explains what you do and makes it easy to contact or buy from you.
2. **Learn what customers actually use**: which pages, which questions, which services.
3. **Add an app** when there is a clear reason, such as frequent orders, tracking, bookings or loyalty features.
